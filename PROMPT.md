# BUILD PROMPT — "DESCENT": An Initiation into the Psychology of C.G. Jung

## 0. Your role

You are a world-class creative developer, experience designer, and writer working as one person. You have Awwwards-level craft in motion and typography, you've read Jung's *Collected Works* and *Memories, Dreams, Reflections*, and you write like a blunt old analyst who doesn't flatter anyone but clearly cares about them.

Build a single, long-scroll, cinematic website that takes a visitor through Jung's psychology **as a transformation**, not as a lecture. It's an ultra landing page in form and an initiation rite in function. By the end, the visitor should feel they went down into something and came back changed.

**North star:** *"I didn't learn about Jung. I went through something."*

---

## 1. The experience in one paragraph

The visitor arrives at the surface: bright, composed, wearing a mask. As they scroll they descend, literally, through the floors of Jung's 1909 dream house, from the elegant upper salon to the cave of bones beneath the cellar. The page goes black. They meet their shadow and sit through a dark night of the soul, which is the centerpiece of the site. Then light returns in the order the alchemists described: black → silver-white → gold → red. They meet the inner figures, learn to read their dreams, draw their own mandala, and hold two opposites until a third thing is born. At the end they come back up to ordinary life. The mask is back, but now they hold it in their hand instead of wearing it. Everything they wrote along the way has been collected in a private "Vessel" they can download.

---

## 2. Structural spine (non-negotiable)

Three metaphors run through the whole site, and all three are Jung's own:

1. **Descent and return (Nekyia / night-sea journey).** The hero is swallowed by the whale, goes into the dark belly, and comes out reborn. A vertical **depth gauge** on the side of the screen reads `0 m` at the top, drops as the visitor scrolls, and climbs back toward the surface in the final act.
2. **Jung's dream of the house (1909, *MDR*).** Upper floor = consciousness. Ground floor = personal unconscious. Roman cellar and prehistoric cave = collective unconscious. Act I physically descends these floors.
3. **The alchemical opus.** The four stages set the site's color arc:
   - **Nigredo** (blackening): shadow, dissolution, the dark night
   - **Albedo** (whitening): moonlight, reflection, the inner others
   - **Citrinitas** (yellowing): dawn, gold, meaning, the Self
   - **Rubedo** (reddening): blood, embodiment, return to life

   The background color, grain, light source, and ambient sound all shift with the stage. A small alchemical glyph marks the current stage in the nav.

---

## 3. The sequence: 5 acts, 16 chapters

Every chapter uses the same anatomy so the rhythm becomes a ritual:

> **(a) The Sting.** One blunt line, set huge, that lands before any explanation.
> **(b) The Teaching.** 150–300 words, clear and concrete, with no jargon it doesn't explain.
> **(c) In His Words.** One verified Jung quote with its source (see §6).
> **(d) The Experience.** One interaction or cinematic moment that makes the idea felt.
> **(e) The Question.** One reflection prompt. The visitor's answer is saved to the Vessel.
> **(f) Go Deeper** (collapsed by default). Nuance, the post-Jungian developments, sources, and further reading.

The Sting lines below are the intended voice. Refine them, but keep them sharp.

### PROLOGUE: The Threshold  *(color: pure black → first light)*

- Black screen. One line fades in: **"Most people spend their whole life avoiding one meeting."** Beat. **"This is that meeting."**
- A button reads **Descend**. A small line suggests "Headphones. 60 minutes. Alone, if you can." The visitor can also choose **"Five Nights" mode**, one act per night with progress saved locally.
- An intention field: *"What brought you here tonight?"* The answer goes into the Vessel.
- **Who Jung was, in 60 seconds:** Swiss psychiatrist (1875–1961), Freud's chosen heir, broke with him in 1912–13, then fell into his own abyss for years and came back with a map. Sting: **"He didn't theorize about the dark. He went through it and took notes."**

---

### ACT I: THE SURFACE  *(daylight → dusk; depth 0 m → 300 m)*

**1. The Persona**
- Sting: **"Your mask isn't a lie. Believing it's all of you is."**
- Teaching: *persona* is Latin for an actor's mask. You need one to function. The danger is identification: the doctor who can't stop being The Doctor, the "nice one" who has never said no. What's left over when the role is taken away?
- Experience: a porcelain mask floats in the hero. As the visitor scrolls, hairline cracks spread across it, and light, or darkness, shows through.
- Question: *"List the roles you play. Which one would you be most terrified to lose?"*

**2. The House (the map of the psyche)**
- Sting: **"You are a house with more floors than you have ever visited."**
- Teaching: retell Jung's 1909 dream in short, cinematic beats: the rococo salon, the medieval ground floor, the Roman cellar, a stone slab with a ring, stairs down to a cave with ancient bones and two skulls. Then give the meaning: the conscious mind, the personal unconscious, and the collective unconscious, the layer shared by all of humanity.
- Experience: **scroll-driven descent through the floors.** The architecture changes style on each level. Parallax layers, dust in the light beams, and the air gets colder (a blue shift) and darker as the visitor goes down. This is the site's signature set piece.
- Question: *"Which room in you have you never gone into?"*

**3. Complexes: What Has You**
- Sting: **"You think you have complexes. The truth is they have you."**
- Teaching: Jung's early fame came from the Word Association Test. Delayed reactions, odd answers, and stumbles revealed emotionally charged clusters, which he called *complexes*: autonomous splinter-selves that take the wheel when you're triggered.
- Experience: **a live recreation of the Word Association Test.** Show 20–30 stimulus words drawn from Jung's original 100-word list ("The Association Method," Clark University lectures, 1909; *CW 2*). Words appear one at a time, the visitor types the first word that comes, and reaction time is measured silently. At the end, show a chart of their slowest responses: *"These are the doors. Something is behind them."* Add a clear note that this is a reflective exercise, not a diagnosis.
- Question: *"Which word made you hesitate? What was the first thing you thought before you typed something else?"*

---

### ACT II: THE DESCENT — NIGREDO  *(black; depth 300 m → 1000 m)*

**4. The Shadow**
- Sting: **"The person you hate most is carrying a letter addressed to you."**
- Teaching: the shadow is everything you refused to be so you could be loved, accepted, or good. It isn't only evil. There is **gold in the shadow** too: disowned anger that is really strength, disowned ambition, sexuality, creativity, needs. Shadow work isn't about becoming dark. It's about becoming *whole*, so the dark stops running you from the basement.
- In His Words: *"One does not become enlightened by imagining figures of light, but by making the darkness conscious."* (*CW 13*, "The Philosophical Tree," §335)
- Experience: **The Mirror.** The visitor names three people who irritate them and the one trait in each that gets under their skin. The page holds those words for a breath, then slowly rewrites each one as *"I am ___."* No commentary. Just the reflection, left there.
- Also include: the **collective shadow** (scapegoats, mobs, war), and an honest short aside called **"Jung's Own Shadow"** covering his failings and his controversial statements in the 1930s. The teacher had a shadow too, which is the point.
- Question: *"What quality did you have to kill in yourself to be loved as a child?"*

**5. Projection and Fate**
- Sting: **"If it keeps happening, it isn't bad luck. It's you, meeting yourself."**
- Teaching: what we don't make conscious we meet outside, as other people, repeating patterns, "fate": the same partner in a different body, the same boss in a different office. Withdrawing a projection is one of the most humbling, and most freeing, acts in a human life.
- In His Words: *"The psychological rule says that when an inner situation is not made conscious, it happens outside, as fate."* (*Aion*, *CW 9ii*, §126). **Use this real wording.** The popular "until you make the unconscious conscious…" version is a paraphrase and must not be presented as Jung's words.
- Experience: **The Loop.** An animation cycles endlessly: a figure, a door, the same scene again. It breaks only when the visitor types the name of their own repeating pattern.
- Question: *"What keeps happening to you? What's the common denominator?"*

**6. THE DARK NIGHT OF THE SOUL**: the centerpiece. Give it the most craft, the most space, and the slowest pacing on the site.
- Sting: **"Something in you is dying. Let it."**
- Teaching (longer here, 500–800 words across several slow screens):
  - Be honest about where the term comes from. *"Dark night of the soul"* is from **St. John of the Cross**, a 16th-century Spanish mystic. Jung described the same territory in his own language: the **nigredo**, the **night-sea journey**, the **nekyia** (descent to the dead), the ego swallowed so that something larger can be born.
  - **Jung's own dark night (1913–1917):** after the break with Freud he was isolated and flooded with overwhelming visions, and he feared for his sanity. He didn't run from it. He wrote down what came, painted it, and spoke with the figures who appeared, among them **Philemon**, his inner teacher. He kept himself anchored in his family and his daily work with patients. The record became *The Red Book*. Late in life he said those years were the most important of his life and that everything afterwards came from them.
  - **What the dark night is:** the collapse of an identity that has become too small. Old meanings stop working. Prayer goes silent, ambition tastes like ash, and the old self can't be put back together. This isn't failure. It's the first stage of the work.
  - **What to do in the dark:** don't rush out. Don't numb it. Don't make it mean you're broken. Keep one anchor in ordinary life, like Jung did. Record the images. Wait. The dark has its own timing.
  - **Blunt truth:** *"Nobody gets out of the dark by thinking positive thoughts. You get out by going through."*
  - **Neurosis vs. legitimate suffering:** *"Neurosis is always a substitute for legitimate suffering."* (*CW 11*, §129). A great deal of misery is what we do to avoid the one pain we actually need to feel.
  - **Roots and heaven:** *"No tree, it is said, can grow to heaven unless its roots reach down to hell."* (*Aion*, *CW 9ii*, §78)
- Experience (make this unforgettable):
  - The screen goes almost fully black. **The cursor becomes a lantern**, and text is visible only inside its small circle of warm light. On touch devices, a finger drag moves the light.
  - Scrolling slows. Text arrives one line at a time.
  - **"Stay."** A single breathing circle, 60 seconds long. Nothing to do and nothing to read. The page asks the visitor to sit in the dark. A quiet "continue" appears only when the minute is up, with an accessible skip always available.
  - The night-sea journey: a faint whale silhouette or leviathan passes far beneath, almost invisible.
- Question: *"What in you is dying right now? What have you been refusing to let die?"*
- **Care note** (small and dignified, not preachy): a spiritual dark night and clinical depression can look alike and can happen at the same time. If the dark includes thoughts of ending your life, reach out now. Include international helplines (e.g. findahelpline.com, and 988 in the US). This also stays reachable from a small persistent footer link: *"If the dark is too much."*

### ACT III: THE INNER OTHERS — ALBEDO  *(moonlight, silver, bone-white; depth 1000 m → 700 m)*

The turn upward begins. The first light is cold moonlight, not sunshine.

**7. Anima and Animus: The Soul-Image**
- Sting: **"The first time you fell in love, you fell in love with a part of yourself you'd never met."**
- Teaching: the inner "other," the soul-figure who appears in dreams, fascinates, and gets projected onto lovers. Present Jung's model clearly, including his four stages of the anima (Eve, Helen, Mary, Sophia). Then say honestly that his gendered framing reflects his era, and that many contemporary Jungians see anima and animus as the psyche's *contrasexual or unknown other* for people of any gender.
- Experience: a moon waxes as the visitor scrolls. The four stages appear as four phases.
- Question: *"Describe the kind of person who always undoes you. What part of you might they be carrying?"*

**8. Dreams: Letters from the Unconscious**
- Sting: **"Every night you receive a letter. Most people never open it."**
- Teaching: for Jung the dream isn't a disguise, as it was for Freud. It's a *spontaneous self-portrait of the psyche's actual situation*, and it often **compensates** for the one-sidedness of the conscious mind. There are no fixed symbol dictionaries. The meaning comes through **personal association** and **amplification** (myth, fairy tale, religion).
- In His Words: the dream is *"a spontaneous self-portrayal, in symbolic form, of the actual situation in the unconscious."* (*CW 8*, §505)
- Experience: **The Dream Workshop.** The visitor enters one dream image and is walked through Jung's method in five steps: (1) the context of your life right now; (2) your personal associations; (3) the mythic amplification (offer a few archetypal echoes for common images like water, house, snake, stranger, or being chased); (4) the compensation question: *"What attitude of your waking mind might this dream be correcting?"*; (5) *"What does the dream want from you?"* Everything is saved to the Vessel.
- Question: *"Write down a dream you've never been able to forget."*

**9. Active Imagination**
- Sting: **"Stop analyzing your inner figures. Talk to them."**
- Teaching: Jung's method for waking dialogue with the unconscious. Let an image arise, let it move, speak to it, and take its answer seriously while staying yourself. This is how the Red Book was made. Add a clear warning: it's powerful, and people who are fragile should do it with a guide.
- Experience: **a guided 5-minute session** with optional ambient audio, a soft timer, gentle step-by-step prompts, and a journal pane where the visitor writes the dialogue.
- Question: *"Who appeared? What did they say?"*

---

### ACT IV: THE GOLD — CITRINITAS  *(dawn, saffron, gold leaf; depth 700 m → 300 m)*

**10. Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious**
- Sting: **"You are not the author of your deepest patterns. You're the latest actor in a very old play."**
- Teaching: archetypes are inherited patterns of perceiving and responding, the self-portraits of instinct. Examples: the Hero, the Great Mother, the Wise Old Man, the Trickster, the Divine Child, the Maiden. Explain the difference between the *archetype itself* (unknowable) and the *archetypal image* (culture-specific). Include the danger of **inflation**: *"When you identify with an archetype you don't become a god. You become possessed."*
- Experience: **a night-sky constellation map.** Each star is an archetype. Tapping one shows two sides: *possessed by it* (unconscious) and *in relationship with it* (conscious).
- Question: *"Which archetype has been running your life without asking?"*

**11. Psychological Types**
- Sting: **"Watch where you fall apart under stress. That's your door."**
- Teaching: the two attitudes (introversion and extraversion, terms Jung made famous) and the four functions: Thinking and Feeling (rational), Sensation and Intuition (irrational). Cover the superior function, and the **inferior function** as the doorway to the unconscious (Marie-Louise von Franz). Note plainly that the MBTI is a later derivative by Briggs and Myers, not Jung's own system.
- Experience: **a four-point compass/mandala** the visitor can rotate. No labeling quiz. Offer reflective prompts that help them *sense* which function is their strongest and which is their weakest.
- Question: *"What do you do badly, childishly, or only under pressure? What might that part of you be trying to become?"*

**12. Synchronicity**
- Sting: **"Sometimes the world answers."**
- Teaching: meaningful coincidence, an acausal connecting principle Jung developed with physicist Wolfgang Pauli. Tell the **golden scarab story**: a patient was recounting a dream of a golden scarab when a rose-chafer beetle tapped on the window. Jung caught it and handed it to her, and her rigid rationalism cracked open. Be honest that this is a hypothesis, not established science, and that seeing signs in everything is inflation, not insight.
- Experience: while the story is being told, a small golden beetle appears and **taps on the glass of the screen** with a soft sound (if audio is on). The visitor can click to "let it in."
- Question: *"When has the world answered you?"*

**13. The Self**
- Sting: **"You are not the center of yourself."**
- Teaching: the Self is the archetype of wholeness, both the center and the whole circle, and the ego is only one small part of it. Jung saw the Self as the psychological image of God (*imago Dei*). Between 1916 and 1918 he drew a mandala nearly every day as a barometer of his inner state, and he came to understand that everything circles around a center.
- In His Words: asked in the 1959 BBC *Face to Face* interview whether he believed in God, he answered: *"I don't need to believe. I know."*
- Experience: **a mandala-drawing canvas** with radial symmetry (6/8/12-fold), a gold-leaf brush, and ink that blooms slowly. The visitor's mandala is saved as an image into the Vessel.
- Question: *"What is the center you keep circling around?"*

---

### ACT V: THE RETURN — RUBEDO  *(deep red, living warmth → dawn; depth 300 m → 0 m)*

**14. The Tension of Opposites and the Transcendent Function**
- Sting: **"Maturity is holding two truths without killing either one."**
- Teaching: enantiodromia means everything pushed to its extreme turns into its opposite (Heraclitus, via Jung). The **transcendent function** is what happens when you hold the tension of opposites consciously, without collapsing into either side. The psyche then produces a *third thing*, a living symbol, that neither side could have produced alone.
- Experience: **Hold.** Two poles sit on either side of the screen, and the visitor names them (e.g. "freedom" / "belonging"). They press and hold. Tension builds visually and audibly. Letting go early collapses everything into one side. Holding long enough (about 10–15 s) makes a new luminous form appear in the center.
- Question: *"What two things inside you are at war? What might the third thing be?"*

**15. Individuation**
- Sting: **"The goal was never to be good. It was to be whole."**
- Teaching: individuation is the lifelong process of becoming the particular person you actually are. It isn't perfection and it isn't individualism. Cover the *coniunctio* (inner marriage of opposites), **the afternoon of life** (midlife as a turn from making a self to meeting the Self), and the **wounded healer**.
- In His Words: *"We cannot live the afternoon of life according to the programme of life's morning; for what was great in the morning will be little at evening, and what in the morning was true will at evening have become a lie."* (*CW 8*, "The Stages of Life," §784)
- Experience: a single line draws itself down the whole page as the visitor scrolls. It turns out to be the path they've taken since the Prologue, and it closes into a circle.
- Question: *"Who are you becoming that you weren't allowed to be?"*

**16. The Return (Epilogue)**
- Sting: **"Now go back. Nothing looks different. Everything is."**
- Teaching: the descent is worthless unless it's lived. Jung built a stone tower at Bollingen with his own hands, chopped wood, cooked, and carved stone. The work ends in ordinary life. The mask comes back, and this time *you're holding it*.
- Experience: the depth gauge reaches `0 m`. The persona mask from Chapter 1 returns, now held in an open hand. The screen warms to dawn.
- **The Vessel:** every answer, the Word Association chart, the dream workshop, and the mandala are shown together as a beautiful personal document. Offer **Download as .md** and **Print / Save as PDF** (with a dedicated print stylesheet). Show a clear promise: *"Nothing you wrote ever left this device."*
- **A letter to yourself**, to be opened in one year.
- Final screen, in silence: **"Vocatus atque non vocatus, deus aderit."** *(Called or not called, the god will be present.)* Explain that Jung carved these words over his door and that they're on his grave.
- Then: further reading and a single quiet link back to the top: **"Descend again."**

## 4. Design system

**Aesthetic:** *The Red Book meets Swiss editorial minimalism.* Illuminated-manuscript warmth (gold leaf, drop caps, jewel tones) on a strict modern grid. The mood is sacred, cinematic, and quiet. Never kitsch, never New-Age stock art, no lotus clip-art, no galaxy gradients.

**Typography**
- Display: a high-contrast serif with presence, e.g. *Cormorant Garamond* or *Instrument Serif*, set very large (the Sting lines can reach 12–18vw on desktop).
- Body: a highly readable book serif, e.g. *EB Garamond* or *Newsreader*, 19–21px, line-height ~1.6, max 62ch.
- Labels/metadata: a small mono (*JetBrains Mono* / *IBM Plex Mono*) for the depth gauge, citations, and chapter numbers. It should feel like case notes.
- Latin inscriptions: small caps with generous tracking.
- Illuminated **drop caps** open each chapter's Teaching.

**Color by stage** (define as CSS tokens and interpolate smoothly on scroll):
| Stage | Background | Ink | Accent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface | warm paper `#EFEAE0` | charcoal | slate blue |
| Nigredo | near-black `#0A0908` | ash `#8C857B` | ember `#C2410C` |
| Albedo | moon-bone `#E6E3DC` / silver night | graphite | silver `#A8B3BF` |
| Citrinitas | deep umber → saffron | parchment | gold leaf `#C9A227` |
| Rubedo | oxblood `#3B0D0D` → dawn | cream | vermilion `#B91C1C` |

**Texture & imagery**
- A subtle animated film grain over everything.
- **Public-domain alchemical engravings** (e.g. *Rosarium Philosophorum*, 1550, which Jung analyzed in *The Psychology of the Transference*; *Splendor Solis*, 1582), sourced from Wikimedia Commons and treated with duotone or halftone to match each stage. **Do not reproduce Red Book imagery**, which is under copyright. Create original art *inspired by* its manuscript style.
- Recurring motifs: ouroboros, lantern flame, moon, scarab, stone, mandala, vessel.

**Motion principles**
- Slow. Think breath-paced easing, not snappy UI. The site should feel like it's *waiting* for you.
- Text reveals line by line or word by word, masked from below.
- Pacing is part of the content: Nigredo is slowest, Citrinitas is the most luminous and expansive, Rubedo is warm and grounded.
- Maybe 2–3 true WebGL moments at most (the hero mask, the lantern darkness, the mandala ink). Everything else should be crafted CSS/GSAP. Restraint makes the big moments hit.

**Sound (optional, OFF by default)**
- A toggle in the corner turns on a generative Web Audio drone per stage: low sub-drone for Nigredo, glassy harmonics for Albedo, warm bells for Citrinitas, a heartbeat-like pulse for Rubedo. Add sound cues for the scarab tap and the "Hold" interaction.

## 5. Tech & architecture

- **Stack:** Next.js (App Router, latest stable) + TypeScript + Tailwind CSS + **GSAP ScrollTrigger** + **Lenis** smooth scroll. **three.js / React Three Fiber** only for the few WebGL moments. Static export is fine, and no backend is needed.
- **Content as data:** keep all chapter copy in structured content files (MDX or TS objects) so the writing can be edited without touching components. Each chapter follows the same schema: `sting, teaching, quote{text, source}, experience, question, deeper[]`.
- **The Vessel:** localStorage wrapped in try/catch, with graceful fallback if storage is unavailable. Nothing is ever sent to a server. No analytics that capture what visitors write. Provide export (.md + print CSS) and a "clear everything" button.
- **Five Nights mode:** remembers the last completed act and offers "Continue from Act III."
- **Navigation:** a minimal side rail with the 5 acts as alchemical glyphs, the depth gauge, a sound toggle, and a chapter index overlay. Visitors must always be able to jump or skip. Guide the scroll, but never trap it.
- **Accessibility:** full `prefers-reduced-motion` support (replace scroll-jacking and WebGL with calm fades), keyboard access for every interaction (e.g. the lantern follows focus or turns into a softly lit column; "Hold" works with the spacebar), WCAG AA contrast even in the dark sections, and alt text for all imagery.
- **Performance:** Lighthouse 90+ on mobile, lazy-load heavy scenes per act, self-hosted subset fonts, and WebGL that degrades gracefully on low-end devices.
- **Mobile:** designed for mobile first, not squeezed onto it. Touch equivalents for every interaction, a 16px minimum gutter, and no horizontal scroll.

## 6. Content rules (strict)

1. **Quotes must be real.** Jung is one of the most misquoted people on the internet. Use only quotes you can trace to the *Collected Works*, *Memories, Dreams, Reflections*, *The Red Book*, the *Letters*, or recorded interviews, and always show the source. If you can't verify a quote, paraphrase it and label it as a paraphrase. **Known fakes to avoid** include *"I am not what happened to me, I am what I choose to become"* and *"Thinking is difficult, that's why most people judge."*
   Verified starting bank (double-check paragraph numbers before shipping):
   - "One does not become enlightened by imagining figures of light, but by making the darkness conscious." (*CW 13*, §335)
   - "The psychological rule says that when an inner situation is not made conscious, it happens outside, as fate." (*CW 9ii*, §126)
   - "No tree, it is said, can grow to heaven unless its roots reach down to hell." (*CW 9ii*, §78)
   - "Neurosis is always a substitute for legitimate suffering." (*CW 11*, §129)
   - "We cannot live the afternoon of life according to the programme of life's morning…" (*CW 8*, §784)
   - "Everything that irritates us about others can lead us to an understanding of ourselves." (*Memories, Dreams, Reflections*)
   - "Who looks outside, dreams; who looks inside, awakes." (letter to Fanny Bowditch, 1916)
   - "The shoe that fits one person pinches another; there is no recipe for living that suits all cases." (*Modern Man in Search of a Soul*)
   - "I don't need to believe. I know." (BBC *Face to Face*, 1959)
   - "Vocatus atque non vocatus, deus aderit." (inscription, Küsnacht; his grave)
2. **Keep Jung distinct from later thinkers.** Clearly separate Jung's own ideas from post-Jungian ones (von Franz, Hillman, Edinger, Woodman, Hollis, Robert A. Johnson) and from pop "shadow work" culture. Credit each accordingly.
3. **Be honest about limits.** Synchronicity is unproven. The anima/animus framing is dated. MBTI isn't Jung's. Jung had real failings. Honesty makes the site *more* powerful, not less.
4. **Copyright:** keep quotes short and cited. Write original prose, and never reproduce long passages. Red Book artwork is off-limits, while pre-1900 alchemical engravings are fair game.

## 7. Voice & tone

- **Second person, present tense, short sentences.** Blunt but warm underneath. Sound like the analyst who tells you the truth because they respect you enough to.
- Every chapter has **one line that stings.** Then it explains itself with care.
- **Banned words and wellness-speak:** *unlock, empower, vibes, manifest, toxic, self-care, healing era, level up, best self, energy* (in the woo sense), *journey* (use at most once on the whole site). No exclamation marks. No emojis.
- Rhythm: big line, silence (whitespace), then the teaching. Let white space do half the work.
- Sample voice lines to calibrate against:
  - "You don't find your shadow. It finds you, usually through someone you can't stand."
  - "The unconscious is not your enemy. Your refusal to listen to it is."
  - "You can't skip the night. You can only make it longer by fighting it."
  - "Wholeness costs you your innocence. It's worth it."

## 8. Care & safety

- A persistent, discreet footer link, **"If the dark is too much,"** opens a calm panel with international crisis resources (findahelpline.com, 988 in the US, local emergency numbers) and one plain sentence: *a dark night is not something you have to survive alone.*
- In the Active Imagination and Dark Night chapters, add a brief note that intense inner work is best done with a trained therapist, especially for anyone with a history of trauma or psychosis.

1. Scaffold + design tokens + typography + the stage-color interpolation + depth gauge.
